# Ancient samples

The individuals behind each source and control in the model, grouped by role.
Sample IDs follow the publications' own identifiers.

## Medieval Turkic reference pool (AD 650–1200)

Central Steppe and Tian Shan individuals used as the incoming-stream source.
Genetically, medieval Turkic populations from these regions sit between
ancient West Eurasian and East Eurasian populations — the pool is a mixture,
not a pure origin.

`DA222`, `DA230`, `SHK001`, `SHK002`, `CHK004`, `DA124`, `DA126`, `DA93`,
`DA94`, `MA2195`

## Controls from the Southern Arc (Lazaridis et al. 2022)

### Çapalıbağ, southwestern Türkiye — 1300–1650 CE

Individuals that postdate the establishment of the Seljuk dynasty. They carry
substantial East Asian ancestry and sit at an intermediate genetic position
between Byzantine Anatolians and their Central Asian ancestors; their dating
lands roughly 12 generations after the Seljuk expansion into Anatolia.

Samples: `I20322_d`, `I20323`, `I20324`, `I20325`, `I20326`, `I20570`,
`I20571`

- Y-DNA: T-L131, J-PF5456, **N-A9416** (Northeast Asian paternal line)
- mtDNA: W9, K1b1c, H13a1a1, HV14a, T2a1a, **A17** (East Eurasian), J2b2,
  H43, K1a, K1a

### Gyumri (Black Fortress cemetery), Armenia — 1665–1950 CE

- `I18247` (mtDNA K1a4b): a post-medieval individual with a genetic profile
  similar to present-day Azerbaijanis, unlike the Late Bronze Age individuals
  from the same site. Used as a control for the eastern, Caucasus-side
  profile, and as evidence that post-medieval Azerbaijani-like ancestry was
  already distinct from what arrived in western Anatolia.
